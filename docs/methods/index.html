Definitions

Anomaly
The difference from the 1991–2020 average for the same calendar month (or season). The only exception is the annual global temperature series, which NOAA NCEI reports relative to 1901–2000.
Relative Oceanic Niño Index (RONI)
NOAA's official El Niño index since 2026: the Niño 3.4 sea surface temperature anomaly measured relative to the tropical-mean anomaly, so that the warming of the whole tropical ocean is not counted as El Niño. Values are overlapping 3-month means, plotted on the middle month (Dec–Feb on January).
El Niño event and strength
Five or more consecutive overlapping 3-month seasons with RONI ≥ +0.5 °C. Strength by peak value: weak (+0.5 to +1.0 °C), moderate (+1.0 to +1.5), strong (+1.5 to +2.0), very strong (≥ +2.0). Winters are classified by their Dec–Feb RONI.
Onset year
The calendar year in which an event's peak season begins; the 1997–98 event has onset year 1997. Events are compared at the same calendar month of their onset years.
Niño regions
Niño 4: 160°E–150°W, 5°S–5°N. Niño 3.4: 170°W–120°W, 5°S–5°N. Niño 3: 150°W–90°W, 5°S–5°N. Niño 1+2: 90°W–80°W, 10°S–0°.
Upper-ocean temperature
GODAS temperature averaged over 2°S–2°N (cosine-weighted) and, where a single value is quoted, over 5–205 m and 180°–80°W. The 20 °C isotherm marks the base of the warm upper layer (the thermocline).
Marine heatwave
Following Hobday et al. (2016): daily SST above the 90th percentile for that day of the year (1991–2020, 11-day window) for at least five consecutive days, using the area mean over 32.5–34.5°N, 117.5–120.5°W (Southern California Bight).
Warm night and hot day
A day with a minimum temperature of at least 65 °F, or a maximum of at least 90 °F, during June–September.
Water year
October to September, labelled by the year in which it ends (as reported by the National Weather Service).

Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms that are underlined on the other pages.

20 °C isotherm
The depth at which the ocean is 20 °C, used to mark the base of the warm upper layer.
anomaly
The difference from normal (the 1991–2020 average) for the same time of year; +2 °C means 2 °C warmer than usual.
El Niño
A periodic warming of the central and eastern equatorial Pacific Ocean, with related changes in winds and rainfall, recurring every two to seven years.
ENSO
El Niño–Southern Oscillation: the cycle of El Niño, neutral and La Niña conditions and the related swing in air pressure across the tropical Pacific.
ERSST
NOAA's Extended Reconstructed Sea Surface Temperature: a monthly 2° record since 1854.
GHCN-Daily
NOAA's Global Historical Climatology Network of daily weather-station records.
GODAS
NOAA's Global Ocean Data Assimilation System, an ocean reanalysis that estimates temperature below the surface.
Hovmöller
A diagram with position on one axis and time on the other, used to follow features as they move.
Kelvin waves
Pulses of warm water that travel east along the equator beneath the surface, crossing the Pacific in about two months.
La Niña
The cool counterpart of El Niño: below-normal ocean temperatures in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific.
marine heatwave
A spell of at least five days when sea surface temperature is above the 90th percentile for the time of year (Hobday et al., 2016).
nClimGrid
NOAA's gridded (about 5 km) monthly temperature and precipitation for the United States since 1895.
Niño 1+2
The region off the coasts of Peru and Ecuador (10°S–0°, 90°W–80°W).
Niño 3
The eastern equatorial Pacific, 5°S–5°N, 150°W–90°W.
Niño 3.4
A region of the central equatorial Pacific (5°S–5°N, 170°W–120°W) whose temperature is the main measure of El Niño.
Niño 4
The western-central equatorial Pacific, 5°S–5°N, 160°E–150°W.
OISST
NOAA's Optimum Interpolation Sea Surface Temperature: a daily 0.25° analysis of satellite, ship and buoy data since 1981.
reanalysis
A best estimate of past ocean or weather conditions, made by combining observations with a numerical model.
Relative Oceanic Niño Index
NOAA's official El Niño index (RONI): the Niño 3.4 anomaly measured relative to the average of the whole tropical ocean, so that general warming is not counted as El Niño. Three-month averages.
RONI
Relative Oceanic Niño Index, NOAA's official El Niño index: the Niño 3.4 anomaly measured relative to the average of the whole tropical ocean. Three-month averages.
Santa Ana
Dry, often strong offshore winds that blow from the interior toward the Southern California coast, mainly in autumn and winter.
thermocline
The layer where temperature drops quickly with depth, separating warm surface water from cold deep water; in the tropics it is marked by the 20 °C isotherm.
trade winds
Steady easterly winds over the tropical Pacific that push warm surface water toward the west.
upwelling
The rise of cold, deep water toward the surface, normally strong along the equator and off South America.
warm pool
The large area of very warm water (above about 28 °C) normally found in the western tropical Pacific.
warm water volume
The volume of water warmer than 20 °C near the equator (5°S–5°N, 120°E–80°W). It builds up before an El Niño and is drained by it.
water year
October to September, labelled by the year in which it ends.

Methods by figure

  • Animations. Monthly fields are shown in sequence; intermediate frames are linear interpolations between consecutive months for smooth playback only and pause on each actual month. The colour scale is “turbo”, a perceptually improved rainbow scale for absolute temperature.
  • “Why this El Niño is different”, time-series animation and interactive explorer. Area-weighted monthly anomalies of ERSST v5 over each Niño region and the GODAS upper-200 m mean (2°S–2°N, 180°–80°W), relative to 1991–2020, for every El Niño since 1950 (1980 for GODAS), aligned on January of the onset year. “At this stage” compares every event in the same calendar month as the latest 2026 value. The statements are recomputed at every update and appear only while the data support them. ERSST and the CPC OISST-based indices differ by up to a few tenths of a degree.
  • Warm water animation. The depth of the 20 °C isotherm is found in each GODAS column by linear interpolation between the 10 m levels (5–205 m); columns warmer than 20 °C throughout are assigned 205 m. Warm water volume is the volume above that depth over 5°S–5°N, 120°E–80°W (the NOAA PMEL definition), computed from GODAS. The normal panel uses the 20 °C depth of the 1991–2020 monthly mean temperature.
  • Layers of warm water. The depths of the 20, 24, 26, 28 and 29 °C isotherms are found in each GODAS column as for the 20 °C isotherm; the volume above each isotherm over 5°S–5°N, 120°E–80°W is summed, and layer volumes are the differences between consecutive isotherms. Sections show the 2°S–2°N mean from 130°E. Water above about 28 °C is commonly taken as the threshold for deep atmospheric convection over the tropical oceans.
  • 3D views. GODAS temperature at 5 m and the depth of the 20 °C isotherm (computed as for the warm water animation) are shown at 1° latitude × 2° longitude from 130°E in the interactive view and ⅔° × 2° over 15°S–15°N in the animation. Cells within two grid points of land are omitted and the fields are lightly smoothed (Gaussian, one grid cell) for display only. Depth is exaggerated by roughly 15,000–20,000 times; the factor is stated on each view.
  • Kelvin waves. Daily sea surface height anomalies from NOAA's blended near-real-time altimetry (CoastWatch ERDDAP, noaacwBLENDEDsshDaily; 0.25°, available from 2017) are averaged over 1°S–1°N every 1° of longitude. The 2017–2024 mean seasonal cycle (monthly means interpolated to days) is removed, which also removes the long-term offset of the record, and a 5-day running mean is applied. Because only eight years are available, this baseline differs from the 1991–2020 normals used elsewhere. Waves are traced automatically as straight eastward paths from 150°E to 90°W, at speeds between 1.8 and 3.2 m/s, along which the mean anomaly is at least +3 cm, at least 25 days apart; a wave is shown once it has crossed.
  • Event development and ranking. RONI for every event since 1950. The current event is completed with NOAA's median forecast and its 5–95% range. Accumulated intensity is the sum of monthly RONI values above +0.5 °C over the event.
  • Comparisons with past events. Each year is shown in the same calendar month as the latest month with data for 2026. The tropical-mean anomaly is the area-weighted mean over 20°S–20°N.
  • Hovmöller diagrams. Longitude–time sections along the equator (2°S–2°N) from January of the onset year to the following January; each row is one month.
  • Forecast verification. Every official CPC strength forecast issued since April 2026, when the RONI-based forecasts began, compared with the category later observed. Lead time is the number of months from the issue month to the centre month of the target season.
  • Global temperature. A record year is one warmer than every earlier year in the NCEI series; highlighted records coincided with an El Niño winter (Dec–Feb RONI ≥ +1.0 °C).
  • Southern California maps. nClimGrid over 32.45–35.15°N, 121.0–114.4°W (land only). Summer is June–September. Long-term change compares 1996–2025 with 1951–1980; trends are least-squares fits from 1950.
  • Southern California precipitation. Region-wide composites use November–March totals in winters with Dec–Feb RONI ≥ +1.5 °C; areas averaging less than 20 mm are left blank. The outlook value for Los Angeles is the highest-probability category covering the city.
  • Stations. GHCN-Daily records with quality-flagged values removed: Los Angeles Downtown/USC (USW00093134), San Diego Lindbergh Field (USW00023188), Santa Barbara (USC00047902), March Air Reserve Base for inland heat (USW00023119; no data 1971–2011) and Lake Elsinore for inland rainfall (USC00042805). Summers need at least 110 of 122 days of data; rainy seasons need at least 145 of 151 days in November–March.

Limitations

  • Only seven strong or very strong El Niño winters have occurred since 1950, so historical relationships rest on small samples and individual events can differ substantially (for example, 2015–16 in Southern California).
  • Values for 2026 are preliminary and may be revised. GODAS is typically released about one month after surface datasets, so subsurface figures may end a month earlier.
  • Absolute temperatures include the long-term warming trend; event strength is therefore judged with the relative index (RONI) and with anomalies, not with absolute temperature.
  • Seasonal outlooks are probabilities for a season and are not forecasts of individual storms or heat events. For day-to-day decisions, use National Weather Service forecasts and warnings.
  • Rainbow colour scales are less distinguishable for readers with colour-vision deficiency; numerical values are given in titles, captions and image descriptions.

Updates and reproducibility

Figures are rebuilt after NOAA's monthly releases (ENSO update on the second Thursday, seasonal outlooks on the third Thursday). All code is in the GitHub repository; python update_site.py --refresh downloads the latest data and rebuilds every figure and page.
