El Niño 2026–27

The 2026–27 El Niño in historical context

A strong El Niño is developing in the equatorial Pacific. These figures compare its surface and subsurface evolution with the strongest events since 1950, using NOAA observations, reanalysis and official forecasts.

Updated October 9, 2026. Data: NOAA observations, reanalysis and official forecasts. Select a figure to view it at full resolution.

What's happening now

Current strength
NOAA's Relative Oceanic Niño Index reached +1.7 °C for Jul–Sep 2026, a strong El Niño. The monthly Niño 3.4 anomaly was +2.8 °C in September.
Compared with past events
In September, Niño 3.4 (+2.8 °C, against +1.8 °C in 1997) and Niño 1+2 (+4.7 °C, against +3.6 °C in 1997) are warmer than in 1982, 1997 and 2015 at the same point in the year.
Below the surface
In August, the upper 200 m of the central and eastern equatorial Pacific was +4.2 °C above normal, compared with at most +2.6 °C (1997) in past very strong events at the same stage.
Forecast
NOAA's October 2026 outlook gives a 100% probability of a very strong event (≥ +2.0 °C), with a median forecast peak of +2.6 °C in Nov 2026–Jan 2027, above the 1950–present record of +2.4 °C (1982–83).
Southern California
Coastal waters have recorded 220 marine heatwave days in 2026 through 23 September, the most on record for the date. NOAA's outlook gives Los Angeles a 50–60% chance of a wetter-than-normal December–February.
