What's happening now

Current strength
NOAA's Relative Oceanic Niño Index reached +1.7 °C for Jul–Sep 2026, a strong El Niño. The monthly Niño 3.4 anomaly was +2.8 °C in September.
Compared with past events
In September, Niño 3.4 (+2.8 °C, against +1.8 °C in 1997) and Niño 1+2 (+4.7 °C, against +3.6 °C in 1997) are warmer than in 1982, 1997 and 2015 at the same point in the year.
Below the surface
In August, the upper 200 m of the central and eastern equatorial Pacific was +4.2 °C above normal, compared with at most +2.6 °C (1997) in past very strong events at the same stage.
Forecast
NOAA's October 2026 outlook gives a 100% probability of a very strong event (≥ +2.0 °C), with a median forecast peak of +2.7 °C in Oct–Dec 2026, above the 1950–present record of +2.4 °C (1982–83).
Southern California
Coastal waters have recorded 220 marine heatwave days in 2026 through 23 September, the most on record for the date. NOAA's outlook gives Los Angeles a 50–60% chance of a wetter-than-normal December–February.

Why this El Niño is different

  1. Warmest El Niño on record at this stage In September, the Niño 3.4 anomaly was +2.9 °C, against a previous high of +2.2 °C (2015–16) among the 22 El Niño events since 1950 at the same point in the year.
  2. Fastest development since 1950 Niño 3.4 has warmed by 3.4 °C since January, more than any previous El Niño over the same months (previous largest: 2.7 °C in 1997–98).
  3. Record warmth across the whole equatorial Pacific At their peaks, past very strong events were strongest either near South America (1982, 1997) or farther west (2015). In September, the current event is the warmest on record at this stage in all four Niño regions (previous high in brackets): Niño 4 +1.2 °C (+1.1 °C), Niño 3.4 +2.9 °C (+2.2 °C), Niño 3 +3.7 °C (+2.8 °C), Niño 1+2 +4.4 °C (+3.8 °C).
  4. Far more heat below the surface In August, the upper 200 m of the equatorial Pacific (180°–80°W) was +4.2 °C above normal, against a previous high of +2.6 °C (1997–98) among the 13 El Niño events since 1980. This reservoir of heat can sustain surface warming over the coming months.
  5. Forecast to set a new record NOAA's October 2026 outlook places the median peak at +2.7 °C on the RONI scale, above the record of +2.4 °C set in 1982–83.
